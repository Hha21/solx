### Notes
- **What binds now.** r0 is slow at every size because it runs on CUDA cores in fp32. At S it is also slow because a few CTAs run long serial loops (137–143 µs flat).
- **What any fast design needs.** FP16x3 MMAs (fp32-accurate split into fp16 parts, 3 products) cost about 96 SM-clk per token, with no GEMM1 recompute.
- **Hidden epilogue cost.** The CUDA-core work after GEMM1 (GELU, square-sums, split into fp16 parts) with exact `erff` costs about 30 instructions per hidden element. That is about 120 issue-clk per token, more than the MMA time. A naive tcgen05 kernel would be bound by this work, not by the MMAs.
- **Key fact: one batch's h fits on chip.** One batch's h is at most 3136 × 2 KB = 6.4 MB. TMEM holds exactly one 128-token × 512-channel fp32 tile per SM. So h can stay on chip at every size if work is scheduled batch by batch; neither recompute nor storing h is forced.
- **Leader fit.** The leader's times fit `10 µs + 5 KB/token at 7 TB/s` within about 5% at every size. That is the traffic of a pipeline that stores h. An on-chip design should beat the leader by about 25% at L and by 30–40% at S/M.
- **Model check.** `predict_score` with S −96%, M −94.7%, L −96% gives a mean of about 0.917.
