### Audit
The weakest premises sit in the leader analysis and in the cost model, not in the precision plan.

1. **The leader's per-workload times come from an assumption, not from data.** The tool assumes the leader scores the same on every workload, so each implied time is just Tsol + 0.171·(Tb − Tsol). This formula exactly reproduces 24.7 µs at 4,56,56 and 21.1 µs at 16,28,28. Three leader stories rest on things this formula creates: the "HW=3136 penalty", the steady 0.723 ns/token slope, and the "5 KB/token at 6.9 TB/s" fit. All three are artefacts of how Tb varies, so they say nothing about how the leader is built.
2. **The leader-gap ceilings are wrong for this problem.** The tool's GFLOP column is 0, so it calls the problem memory-bound and says precision does not matter (ceiling 0.994). The problem card's Tsol of about 29 µs at 64,56,56 is also wrong: the value recovered from the portal is 67.45 µs (0.334 ns/token).
3. **The GELU, not the tensor cores, is the bottleneck in the on-chip designs.** The sm_100a assembly for `erff` GELU is 1 special-function-unit instruction plus about 28 ordinary instructions per element. That is about 112 issue-clocks per token, against 96 clocks of FP16x3 tensor-core work. Adding +b1, sum of squares and the hi/lo split gives about 136 clocks per token. So I5, I21 and I24, which assume tensor-core speed plus a 2.5 µs epilogue, are too optimistic. I11's claim that the special-function unit binds is also wrong; the ordinary-instruction count does. Cutting that count (I27) is what makes those designs possible. For the same reason recompute (H5) costs more than "+50% FLOPs": it runs the GELU a second time too.
4. **Precision (H2, I15): FP16x3 passes on real hardware, but the error is 2.5× what the emulation predicted.** I ran the tensor-core GEMMs through Triton and confirmed the generated code uses tcgen05 MMAs. Raw max error is 5.7e-3, not 2.3e-3, so the "82% of atol" margin figure is the wrong measure. The real test is the worst error/tolerance ratio, since the rtol term saves the large outputs:
   - single accumulator: 0.74–0.78
   - separate accumulator for the hi·lo terms: 0.44
   - fp32 add per K-block: 0.29
   GEMM2 causes most of it (0.70 alone, against 0.25 for GEMM1). No element failed in 19 runs over 7 shapes. 3xTF32 on hardware is worse, at 9e-3 max error.
5. **I23's speed claim fails its own test.** A skeleton with no MMAs, no conversion and no GELU already takes 6.8 µs rented at 1,28,28: 4.8 µs to load and store, 1.45 µs for the grid barrier, 0.55 µs for the 16-way red.add. I23 promised 6–7 µs for the whole kernel. Expect about 10 µs rented, which only matches the leader.

The probe budget ran out before the L2 read-back test for I26, I12 and I3, so that premise is still untested.
